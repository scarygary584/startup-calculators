Cash left is not the same as final taxable profit

A solo mower may physically keep most of a $60 payment after buying gas. That is the useful first number. A complete business picture still needs insurance, taxes, mower replacement, repairs, advertising, vehicle wear, unpaid estimates, and weather downtime.

Why route density matters

Six nearby lawns can create a strong day. Six lawns scattered across town can burn time and gas between jobs. Change the total job time and gas fields to make the estimate fit the route.

FAQ

If I charge $60 and spend $5 on gas, did I earn $55?

You have $55 in cash left from that job before equipment wear, taxes, insurance, and other overhead. The calculator shows that number separately so it is not hidden behind an artificial owner-labor expense.

Why include an equipment reserve?

Mower blades, oil, belts, tires, repairs, and eventual replacement are real costs even when they are not paid on every individual job.

Is owner earnings the same as taxable profit?

Not necessarily. The result is a job-level planning estimate. Taxes, insurance, advertising, vehicle costs, and other overhead may still need to be deducted.
