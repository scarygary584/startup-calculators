Numbers from the featured lemonade business

The operator reported selling a 32-ounce fresh-squeezed lemonade for $7 with about $1.25 in ingredients and packaging cost. That included the lemon, simple syrup, cup, lid, straw, and ice.

He reported roughly $1,200 to $1,500 in daily sales at one farmers market and about $2,500 per day at a stronger Dallas market. The Dallas booth fee was reported as about $300 total for the weekend, including electricity.

He estimated basic operational startup costs at roughly $1,500, although branding, decor, carts, trailers, storage, and production equipment can raise that considerably.

Exceptional examples included nearly $6,000 in three hours at an eclipse event. That is best treated as an unusual peak event, not a normal default.

Other ways a drink cart can earn

The featured operator also reported catering jobs commonly ranging from about $1,000 to $3,000, a four-hour catering example totaling about $2,900, and cart-only rentals around $500, sometimes with a separate delivery and pickup charge.

This calculator focuses on direct drink sales at markets and festivals. Catering and cart rental can be additional revenue streams rather than replacements for the event math above.

How the calculator works

Event revenue = event days × drinks sold per day × average sale.

Variable cost includes product cost per drink and card-processing fees.

Event profit = revenue − product costs − card fees − booth fee − labor − permits, travel, parking, and miscellaneous costs.

Break-even drinks = fixed event costs ÷ contribution profit per drink.

FAQ

What drinks fit this calculator? Use it for lemonade, limeade, agua fresca, iced tea, flavored soda, coffee, mocktails, energy drinks, snow cones, or another cart with a per-sale product cost.

Why include break-even drinks? It shows how many sales are needed before the cart begins paying you instead of merely covering the event.

Should I use the $7 and $1.25 defaults? Only as a starting example. Enter your actual selling price, recipe, packaging, ice, waste, labor, card fees, and event charges.

Are permits included? The calculator has a field for permits and miscellaneous event costs, but local food handling, health, sales-tax, insurance, fire, and vendor requirements vary.

Chris Koerner-style reality check: The revenue and profit examples on this page come from one featured entrepreneur describing his own experience. They are not typical results, promises, or guarantees. A great event, weather, location, crowd, product, speed of service, branding, staffing, and experience can change the numbers dramatically. Do your own research, follow local food and vendor rules, and run conservative numbers before spending money.
