How to use the washer and dryer rental calculator

The starting example uses 67 rented sets at $150 each for $10,050 in monthly revenue before expenses. These are adjustable planning inputs, not verified results from the featured business. Review storage, transport, and processing costs for your fleet size.

Enter the monthly price for one washer-and-dryer set and the number of sets currently rented. Add your acquisition, delivery, repair, storage, truck, fuel, processing, churn, and fleet-growth assumptions.

The calculator shows operating profit separately from cash after new set purchases. Acquisition and delivery costs for newly purchased sets reduce the cash result, not operating profit. Enter only the sets you expect to buy this month in “New sets added per month”; use zero if you are making no purchases. Include replacement purchases here too.

Results are planning estimates, not guaranteed income. Use quotes and actual operating records before buying inventory.

Washer and dryer rental profit formula

Monthly revenue = monthly rental price × rented sets.

Repair reserve = repair reserve per set × rented sets.

New-set investment = (acquisition cost + delivery/install cost) × new sets added.

Operating profit before owner pay and tax = revenue − repair reserve − storage − truck/fuel − processing.

Monthly cash after new set purchases = operating profit − new-set investment.

Annualized operating profit estimate = monthly operating profit × 12, assuming the same activity and costs each month. New-set purchases are not deducted from this annualized figure.

Startup payback estimate = (acquisition cost + delivery/install cost) × rented sets ÷ monthly operating profit. This simplified estimate covers the entered rented fleet and its initial delivery cost; it does not account for ongoing fleet purchases, idle inventory, or other startup costs. It is not a forecast of when all invested cash will be recovered.

The result does not automatically include every tax, insurance premium, unpaid balance, or owner wage. Add omitted expenses by increasing the closest monthly-cost input or subtracting them separately.

Costs to verify before buying a fleet

  • Used or refurbished appliance purchase price and expected useful life
  • Hoses, cords, vents, pans, dollies, stairs, delivery labor, and installation time
  • Storage, truck, fuel, insurance, payment processing, and collection costs
  • Cleaning, repair calls, parts, retrieval, damage, theft, and disposal
  • Idle sets between customers and seasonal or tenant-turnover gaps
  • Business registration, contracts, rental taxes, and local requirements

Example: about $10,000 in monthly revenue

At the default inputs, 67 rented sets at $150 per month produce $10,050 in monthly revenue. The $1,005 repair reserve, $200 storage cost, $250 truck/fuel cost, and $50 processing cost total $1,505, leaving $8,545 in operating profit before owner pay and tax. Buying five sets at $250 each plus $75 delivery/install per set uses another $1,625, leaving $6,920 in monthly cash after new set purchases.

With no new purchases, operating profit stays at $8,545 and cash after purchases is also $8,545 at these same rental and operating-cost inputs. Changing new-set purchases alone changes cash after purchases; it does not change operating profit or the displayed startup payback. Newly purchased sets do not automatically increase rented sets or revenue. Update “Number of rented sets” separately to model a different active fleet.

Price for service, not just equipment

A monthly rental price may need to cover delivery, installation, replacement response, collection risk, retrieval, and customer support—not merely the appliance purchase price. Compare monthly price, minimum term, delivery fee, deposit, and repair responsibilities together.

Questions to answer before starting

Who owns the appliances during the rental? Who is allowed to install them? What happens after nonpayment, damage, a leak, or a move-out? How quickly will you repair or replace a failed machine? A written rental agreement and appropriate insurance matter as much as the calculator.

Landlord or property-manager relationships may reduce customer-acquisition work, but they can also create response-time and billing expectations. Confirm the economics and responsibilities in writing.

Washer and dryer rental business FAQ

How do you calculate washer and dryer rental profit?

Estimate monthly rental revenue, then subtract repair reserves, storage, truck and fuel costs, and payment processing to get operating profit before owner pay and tax. Subtract newly purchased sets and their delivery/install costs separately to get monthly cash after new set purchases.

How much should be reserved for repairs?

Start with a conservative per-set monthly reserve based on equipment age and condition. Replace the default with actual service history once you have enough rentals to measure it.

What costs are easy to miss?

Delivery labor, stairs, hoses and cords, storage, missed payments, retrieval, cleaning, damage, theft, truck time, insurance, taxes, and idle inventory are commonly overlooked.

Can landlord partnerships help?

They may create repeat rental opportunities, but written terms, response times, installation responsibilities, and payment arrangements should be clear.

Cost and workload assumptions

Operating profit excludes new-set purchases; monthly cash after new set purchases subtracts their acquisition and delivery/install costs separately. Revenue uses the entered active rented sets for a full month. Churn is a separate planning count and does not automatically reduce revenue or add costs. Reduce active rented sets to model vacancies. The delivery/install input applies only to newly purchased sets; account separately for retrieval, redelivery of existing sets, and other costs not included in the inputs.
